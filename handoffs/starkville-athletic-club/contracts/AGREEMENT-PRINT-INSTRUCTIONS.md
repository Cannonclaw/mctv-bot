## The ask, in one line

> "One page, letter, portrait, full colour, actual size — on your heaviest
> **uncoated** stock. Six copies. No coating, no finishing."

That is the whole job.

---

## The one thing that matters most

**Uncoated. Not gloss, not silk, not "premium."**

Ask a print counter for "good paper" and they reach for coated gloss, because that
is what looks expensive on a flyer. **A pen skates on gloss.** Joe is going to
write his bank routing number and sign his name on this page. On a coated sheet a
ballpoint skips, gel ink beads up and smears under his hand, and the signature
looks like it was written on a moving truck.

Say the word *uncoated* out loud. If they offer to show you a sample, take the
matte uncoated one and drag a pen across it before you agree.

## Specs

| Spec | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Pages | 1 |
| Size | US Letter, 8.5 × 11 in |
| Orientation | Portrait |
| Colour | Full colour — the navy band and gold rules are the whole look |
| **Stock** | **Uncoated**, 28–32 lb bond / 70–80 lb text. 100 lb uncoated text if they have it. |
| Shade | Bright white or natural white. **Not cream** — it fights the form's own cream panels. |
| Scaling | **Actual size / 100%** — *not* "fit to page" |
| Sides | Single-sided |
| Finishing | None. No staple, no fold, no lamination. |
| Copies | 6 |

**Why six.** One for Joe to sign, one for him to keep, one for our file, and three
spares. He will change his mind about which box to tick at least once, and a
crossed-out checkbox on a payment authorization looks sloppy. Blank copies are
cheap; driving back is not.

---

## Also worth avoiding

**"Fit to page."** Same failure as the deck. The form is drawn to the sheet with
its own margins; shrink it and you get a second white border and the fields land
in the wrong place for a pen.

**Cardstock.** 80 lb *cover* or heavier feels impressive and is wrong here — it is
stiff to write on without a hard surface under it, and it does not fold into a
folder. Text weight, not cover weight.

**Any kind of coating or "protective finish."** It is a form. It gets written on.

**Printing it pre-filled.** Print it blank. Every field gets completed by hand in
front of him, including the package checkbox and the amount authorized. A
pre-ticked box reads as presumptuous and, on a payment authorization, it is the
kind of thing that gets argued about later.

## After it is signed

The completed page carries **bank account and card details**. Treat it accordingly:

- Do not leave it face-up on a counter, a passenger seat, or a desk at the club.
- Do not photograph it and text it to anybody.
- Scan or hand it to accounting directly, then keep the paper copy in the file.
- If a copy has to move by email, it goes encrypted, not as a plain attachment.

The form says *Confidential* in the footer for this reason.

---

## If something is not available

- **No uncoated in a heavier weight** — take standard 24 lb uncoated over any
  coated stock at any weight. Writability beats heft every time on this page.
- **Colour printer down** — greyscale is acceptable *here*, unlike the deck. The
  form is legible and legally complete in black and white; the navy band just
  goes grey. Print it and move.
- **Shop is backed up** — this is one page. Ask if they can run it at the
  self-serve machine. Colour, letter, actual size, uncoated tray.
