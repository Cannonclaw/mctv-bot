## Why the email says what it says

**The apology is one clause and then it moves on.** Anything longer reads as a problem where
there is not one. He called us.

**It does not pitch.** It says what the meeting is about and stops. The sheet carries the
three offers; the email carries the tone.

**No hours are quoted back to him.** Directory listings show staffed hours alongside 24-hour
key card access and we have not confirmed which is right (see PITCH-BRIEF §2). "Current at
whatever hour somebody walks in" makes the after-hours argument without asserting his
schedule — get the real answer in the room.

**The schedule request is a soft close.** Asking for his class times gets him doing something
before the meeting, and it is the fastest way to make the board feel like his.

**Asking for a direct address is worth a line.** The general inbox is where the March 2025
appointment went sideways. A reply-with-your-address is a cheap way out of that.

## What is deliberately NOT in it

Per the offer ladder in `PITCH-BRIEF.md` §4, these are meeting material, not email material:

- **Any price.** The $199 block, the $599 territory, the MSU package — all of it stays out.
  A number in writing before the room is a number he negotiates against instead of hearing.
- **Category exclusivity, and "there is not one gym on a Starkville screen."** That is the
  close. It is the strongest card we hold and it should not be spent in a cold email.
- **CPMs and comparison tables.** Nothing in this email invites a spreadsheet.
