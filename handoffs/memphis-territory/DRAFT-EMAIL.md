## Why it's built this way

**No numbers that can become a clause.** No firm screen count anywhere — not in the note, not in
the PDF. The build intent is one sentence with no figures attached: start with a couple of markets,
real numbers once the boundaries are clear. Any number offered before we know what we are being
offered becomes the build-out obligation.

**The attachment is named and located.** "The map on page two" gets it opened. A vague "see
attached" often doesn't. Two pages also reads as a summary rather than a proposal, which suits a
first contact.

**The Tennessee question is framed as a reconciliation, not a challenge.** "I want to be sure I'm
looking at the same footprint you are" invites him to explain the boundary. Asking "why is she
operating outside her territory?" would put him on the defensive about his own operator.

**One ask, not five.** Exclusivity, the build-out obligation, which agreement DeSoto falls under,
and the NTV360 license export all matter — but they're follow-ups once he's answered the boundary
question. Five questions in a first email reads as diligence; one reads as interest.
