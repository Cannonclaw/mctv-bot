# Memphis / DeSoto Territory — Decision Brief

**Prepared for:** Creed Cannon, MCTV Elite Advertising
**Occasion:** Call with Don Winfrey (n-Compass / NTV360) re: Memphis-area territory availability
**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Decision deadline:** Reply to Don within 24 hours
**Status:** Research complete — recommendation below, three items still need Don to answer

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Take DeSoto County. Take the paper rights to Marshall and Tate County at the same time. Build in
Olive Branch and Hernando first, not Southaven.**

DeSoto County is the single best advertiser market in Mississippi and it is not close. It has the
**highest median household income of any county in the state (~$85,500)**, the **4th-most businesses
of any county in the state**, and **~198,000 people** — more than Lafayette County and Lee County
*combined*, which is our entire Oxford + Tupelo footprint today.

There is no second DeSoto anywhere in North Mississippi. The alternative markets near Tupelo and
Oxford were checked (Section 6) and every one of them is small, flat, or shrinking. If this
territory is genuinely available for free, it is worth taking even if we build slowly.

**The catch:** "free territory" is free the way a puppy is free. The cost is not the rights, it is
the build — hardware, install, and a rep living in DeSoto County. Get the three answers in Section 7
from Don before committing to a build-out schedule.

## 2. What we could and could not confirm about DeSoto Local

The incumbent is **Desoto Local, Inc.** — an authorized n-Compass TV / NTV360 dealer, same platform
we run on. Principal is **Brandy Faulkner** (listed as Business Partner). They market under
desotolocal.biz, run a host-recruitment page at `/host/`, and describe their footprint as
"restaurants to gyms, doctors' offices to auto repair" throughout DeSoto County. They are already
in SOUL.md as a known competitor ("DeSoto County. Small, hyper-local.").

**We could not get a venue-level location list, and it does not exist publicly.** Their site
returns 403 to automated fetches and the domain is blocked by this environment's egress policy.
Their chamber listings (Horn Lake, Southaven) are also 403. No aggregator publishes n-Compass
venue lists. I checked.

**The authoritative list already exists and Don can hand it over in five minutes.** Every screen is
a license in NTV360. Ask Don for an export of Desoto Local's active licenses — venue name, address,
license ID, install date, contract end date. That single export answers "what are their locations"
completely and accurately, which no amount of web research will. We may even be able to see part of
it from our own NTV360 dashboard, since we are on the same platform.

**Do not build the plan on the incumbent failing.** The report that she may be undercapitalized is
secondhand and unverifiable, and it is the wrong thing to lean on. Either we get written exclusivity
or we plan to coexist and win on execution. Both are fine. Assuming she folds is not a plan.

## 3. Why DeSoto County is worth it

| Metric | DeSoto County | Lafayette (Oxford) | Lee (Tupelo) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Population | **197,918** (2025 est) | 60,289 (2025) | 83,343 (2020) |
| Median household income | **~$85,500 — #1 in MS** | $64,334 | $66,257 (Tupelo city) |
| Business count rank in MS | **4th of 82 counties** | — | — |
| Growth | +14.1% (2015–2025), 25 straight years | — | — |

DeSoto grew from 185,314 (2020 census) to 197,918 (2025 estimate). Growth is driven by in-migration,
not births — natural change actually went negative in 2024–25. That matters for us: people moving in
are forming new households, shopping for new dentists, gyms, restaurants and auto shops. That is
exactly the moment indoor billboards work.

**Positioning shift required.** In Oxford we sell "we are the only network in town." That does not
work here. DeSoto County sits inside the **Memphis DMA (#51 nationally, 666,300 TV households)**, so
local advertisers already have Memphis TV, radio, and outdoor in front of them. Our pitch in DeSoto
is the *other* one from the deck: **a fraction of Memphis media cost, and it actually reaches
DeSoto County instead of wasting 80% of the buy on Memphis proper.** Helpfully, Memphis DMA TV
households are down ~1% year over year — cord-cutting is pushing local dollars toward DOOH, which
is our tailwind, not our problem.

## 4. Where to build first — ranked

### Tier 1 — start here

**Olive Branch — pop 47,819.** Officially named Mississippi's #1 "boomtown" and repeatedly listed
among the nation's fastest-growing suburbs with home values under $500k. Highest-income slice of the
highest-income county in the state. Sits on I-269 directly adjacent to the Marshall County job boom
(Section 5), so those paychecks get spent here. A mixed-use development ("Cascades") is in the works.
*This is the flagship.*

**Hernando — pop 19,165, +10.96% since the 2020 census (+1.81%/yr).** The **fastest-growing** of the
DeSoto cities in percentage terms, and the county seat. Critically, Hernando has a **walkable
courthouse square** — restaurants, salons, professional offices ringing a town center. That is the
Oxford playbook exactly, and it is the one we have already proven at 75 screens. Small enough to
own outright and quickly.

### Tier 2 — highest venue density, but most contested

**Southaven — pop 58,551, largest city in the county.** Silo Square alone has ~29 businesses in a
town-square format, plus Tanger Outlets and the Landers Center (Memphis Hustle, concerts). On paper
the richest venue inventory in the county. In practice this is almost certainly where the incumbent
is most dug in, and where new host agreements will be hardest. Go here *second*, with the Olive
Branch and Hernando networks already built as proof.

### Tier 3 — fill-in, not a launch

**Horn Lake — pop 26,738, growth essentially flat (-0.01%/yr).** Real population, no momentum.
**Walls (1,437)** and **Nesbit (unincorporated, ~6,700–9,500 depending on boundary)** are rounding
errors. Add these once a rep is already driving the county.

> Note on sources: published growth rates for Olive Branch vary a lot by source (one puts it near
> +0.5%/yr, another cites ~20% over two years) depending on whether they use city limits or
> annexation-adjusted boundaries. The 2026 population estimates above are consistent across sources;
> treat the growth *percentages* as directional.

## 5. Marshall County — take the rights, hold the capex

Marshall County is having a genuinely enormous industrial run:

| Project | Investment | Jobs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Jabil** (announced Jul 2026) | $119M | **~2,200** | Renovating an existing 1.5M sq ft plant for data-center infrastructure |
| **Jabil** (Sept 2025) | $70M | — | X-ray sterilization site near Byhalia |
| **Amazon** | — | ~1,000 | 930,000 sq ft cross-dock, Chickasaw Trails Industrial Park, Byhalia |
| **Baxter International** | $11.6M | 105 | US distribution hub, I-269 / Hwy 302 |

That is ~3,300+ announced jobs in a county whose towns are tiny and getting smaller:

- **Byhalia: population 1,417**, median household income $38,500
- **Holly Springs: population 6,285, declining -1.83%/yr**, median household income $45,462

**Read that carefully — it is the whole strategic point.** The jobs are in Marshall County. The
*households and the retail spend are not.* Those workers commute in from DeSoto County and spend
their money in Olive Branch and Southaven. Screens belong where people shop, eat, and wait — not
where they clock in.

**So: ask Don for Marshall County rights on paper, and put zero hardware there for now.** If the
rights are free, they are cheap insurance against someone else claiming the corridor. Revisit if
rooftops actually follow the jobs.

## 6. The alternative markets you asked about — honest answer

You asked whether somewhere in the Tupelo / Oxford / North Mississippi range might be a better
growth play. I checked all of them. **None of them compare, and most are not growing at all.**

| Market | Population | Trend |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Senatobia (Tate Co.) | 8,499 | +0.31%/yr — flat |
| Batesville / Panola Co. | 33,208 (county, 2020) | flat |
| New Albany | 7,784 | +0.36%/yr — flat |
| Pontotoc (city / county) | 6,256 / 32,201 | +1.62% city, +0.33% county |
| Corinth / Alcorn Co. | 34,740 → 34,569 (2020→2025) | **shrinking** |
| Holly Springs | 6,285 | **shrinking, -1.83%/yr** |

Pontotoc County has Toyota Blue Springs (~2,250 employees) and that is a real anchor, but the county
is only growing 0.33%/yr — the plant is 15 years old and its growth is already priced in.

**The much better "alternative" is not a new town. It is densifying Tupelo.**

Run the per-capita math on our own network:

- **Oxford: 75 screens / 27,135 people = 1 screen per 362 residents**
- **Tupelo: 25 screens / 38,091 people = 1 screen per 1,524 residents**

Tupelo is a **larger city than Oxford** and we have **one-third the screens**. At Oxford's density
Tupelo would carry ~105 screens. That is roughly **80 screens of headroom in a market we already own,
with no territory negotiation, no new rep, and hosts who already know the brand.** Columbus (2
screens) and West Point (1 screen) are similarly unfinished.

Frame it this way: DeSoto is the *land grab*, Tupelo is the *free money*. They are not competing
priorities and you should not trade one for the other — but if capital is tight this quarter,
Tupelo densification returns faster.

## 7. Three things to get from Don before you commit

Ask these in the reply. Do not accept a handshake "it's available."

1. **What exactly is available, in writing?** Full exclusive rights to DeSoto County, or coexistence
   with Desoto Local? If the incumbent still holds active licenses, we need her venue list and
   contract end dates. "Available to some extent" is not a territory grant.
2. **What is the export?** Request the NTV360 license export for Desoto Local — venue, address,
   license ID, install date, contract end. This is the location list you asked me for, and Don is
   the only person who can produce it.
3. **What is the build-out obligation?** Free territory almost always carries a performance clause —
   X screens live in Y months or the rights revert. Get the number before agreeing, because that
   number is the actual price.

Also worth asking: does the grant include Marshall and Tate County, and do we get right of first
refusal on the rest of the Memphis-adjacent Mississippi counties?

## 9. Risks to keep in front of you

- **The build is the cost, not the rights.** A credible DeSoto launch is 40–60 screens plus a rep on
  the ground in the county. Model that before signing a build-out clause.
- **Coexistence on the same platform is messy.** If Desoto Local stays active on NTV360, we are
  competing for the same host agreements under a confusingly similar pitch. Venues will notice.
- **This is a DMA market, not a college town.** Our Oxford motion — own the square, be the only game
  — does not transfer. DeSoto needs the cost-versus-Memphis-media pitch and a rep who can sell
  against real alternatives.
- **Do not plan around the incumbent running out of money.** Unverified, and irrelevant if we get
  exclusivity in writing.
- **Screens are not revenue.** Our pricing is per-advertiser ($350–$1,300/mo by tier), so what
  actually pays is advertisers sold, not screens hung. DeSoto's appeal is that it has the 4th-largest
  business base in the state to sell *to* — but it still has to be sold.
