**Status:** Iteration 1 — corridor structure and node-level screen targets established
**Last updated:** 2026-08-04

---

## How to read these maps

MCTV screens don't sell by city, they sell by **node** — a commercial cluster where the right venue
categories sit close enough together that one rep can walk it and one advertiser buy feels local.
Every market below is broken into nodes, each with a screen target, the venue categories that
actually exist there, and why it ranks where it does.

Screen targets are grounded in our own density benchmarks:

| Market | Screens | Population | Density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxford | 75 | 27,135 | **1 per 362** ← the proven ceiling |
| Starkville | 30 | ~25,000 | ~1 per 833 |
| Tupelo | 25 | 38,091 | **1 per 1,524** ← under-built |
| Columbus | 2 | — | unfinished |
| West Point | 1 | — | unfinished |

DeSoto County at Oxford density would be ~547 screens. That is not a Phase 1 number — it is the
size of the prize. The targets below total **~65–80 screens across DeSoto**, which is a credible
18-month build and enough inventory to sell the county as a real network.

## MARKET 1 — Olive Branch (Phase 1 flagship)

**Pop 47,819 · Mississippi's #1 "boomtown" · highest-income slice of the highest-income county in MS**

Target: **20–24 screens**

| # | Node | Geography | Screen target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Goodman Road @ Pleasant Hill** | MS-302, businesses both sides, Target + shopping centers | **8–10** | The primary retail spine of Olive Branch and northern DeSoto County. National tenants, heavy foot traffic. Anchor node — build here first. |
| 2 | **Craft Road / I-22 corridor** | Craft Road exit off I-22, mixed-use + industrial-adjacent | **4–5** | Catches the **Marshall County commuter flow** (Jabil, Amazon, Baxter). These are the paychecks that don't get spent in Byhalia. |
| 3 | **Old Towne Main Street** | Between Hwy 305, Hwy 178, and Goodman Rd | **4–5** | Walkable historic district — gifts, collectibles, antiques, local restaurants. **This is the Oxford Square analog.** Our single most proven venue environment. |
| 4 | **The Cascades** | New mixed-use: restaurant parcels, 100 townhomes, 58 cottage lots, 70-acre park | **3–4** | Under construction. Get host agreements signed *before* tenants open — first-mover on a brand-new development is the cheapest inventory we will ever buy. |

**Venue category mix to work:** restaurants → salons/barbershops → gyms → medical/dental → auto repair.
Same order that worked in Oxford.

**Why Olive Branch is the flagship and not Southaven:** highest household income, fastest economic
momentum, adjacent to the job boom, and — critically — likely the *thinnest* incumbent presence,
since a small operator starting in DeSoto County almost certainly started in Southaven.

## MARKET 2 — Hernando (Phase 1 co-launch)

**Pop 19,165 · +10.96% since the 2020 census (+1.81%/yr) — fastest-growing in the county · county seat**

Target: **12–15 screens**

| # | Node | Geography | Screen target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Courthouse Square / Commerce Street** | Historic downtown | **6–8** | Boutiques, retailers, restaurants ringing a walkable square. Plus the **Hernando Farmers Market every Saturday, May–October**, with live music and family activities on the square. This is Oxford's playbook running on a smaller stage — and we already know it works. |
| 2 | **McIngvale Road / I-55 Exit** | Off I-55, south of E. Commerce St | **4–5** | Existing 68,865 sq ft shopping center plus new retail buildings leasing now. **McIngvale Square** is adding 27,000 sq ft of retail/restaurant/office/residential on 4 acres, explicitly to pull high-end dining east toward the interstate. Another get-in-early node. |
| 3 | **Hwy 51 corridor** | North–south through town | **2–3** | Fill-in. Service businesses and auto. |

**Why Hernando co-launches with Olive Branch:** it is small enough to *own outright* in one build.
Owning a county seat's entire square is a far better sales story than being 15% of Southaven, and it
gives the rep a finished reference market to show Olive Branch and Southaven prospects.

## MARKET 3 — Southaven (Phase 2)

**Pop 58,551 · largest city in DeSoto County · richest venue inventory · most contested**

Target: **25–30 screens**

| # | Node | Geography | Screen target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Silo Square** | S of Goodman Rd, W of Getwell, E of Tchulahoma | **8–10** | $200M town-square development, ~29 businesses. Wine bar, music hall, barber shop, restaurants, South Point Grocery. Purpose-built walkable retail — **the single best venue cluster in the entire county.** |
| 2 | **Goodman Road (MS-302) spine** | Primary retail corridor, seconds off I-55 | **8–10** | The city's main commercial artery. National tenants and heavy traffic, but also the deepest bench of independent service businesses. |
| 3 | **Snowden District** | BankPlus Amphitheater, Snowden Grove Park, Shoppes at Snowden Grove (Kroger) | **4–6** | **Sleeper node.** Snowden Grove is a youth baseball complex — tournament weekends park hundreds of families for hours with nothing to do between games. Highest captive dwell time in the county. Amphitheater draws national acts. |
| 4 | **Airways Blvd / Church Rd @ I-55/I-69** | Tanger Outlets (70 stores, 330k sq ft) | **3–4** | Huge draw pulling shoppers from Tennessee, but tenants are national chains that buy media at corporate. **Target the surrounding service businesses, not the outlet tenants.** |
| 5 | **Landers Center** | 4560 Venture Dr — 10,000-seat arena, Memphis Hustle (NBA G-League) | **2–3** | Event-driven. Best as a marquee host logo for the sales deck as much as for impressions. |

**Sequencing note:** go here *second*, with Olive Branch and Hernando already live. Walking into
Southaven with two finished networks and a real traction report beats walking in with a pitch deck.

## MARKET 4 — Horn Lake / Walls / Nesbit (Phase 3 fill-in)

**Horn Lake 26,738 (flat, -0.01%/yr) · Walls 1,437 · Nesbit unincorporated (~6,700–9,500)**

Target: **8–10 screens**

Nodes: **Goodman Road west**, **Church Road**, **Tulane Road**.

Real households and real businesses, but no growth story. Add these once a rep is already driving
the county weekly — the marginal cost of servicing them is near zero at that point, and the extra
screens push the county-wide network count over the threshold where the top pricing tier ($1,300/mo,
75+ screens) becomes sellable.

## MARKET 5 — Tupelo densification (parallel track — no territory negotiation required)

**Pop 38,091 · larger than Oxford · currently 25 screens (1 per 1,524) vs Oxford's 1 per 362**

Target: **+40–50 screens** (toward ~70–75 total)

| # | Node | Geography | Screen target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **NMMC medical district** | North Mississippi Medical Center, 640 beds | **12–15** | **The best untapped node in the whole network.** Largest non-metro hospital in America; the largest private not-for-profit hospital in Mississippi. Serves 730,000 people across 24 counties in MS, AL, and TN. The surrounding medical office buildings are wall-to-wall waiting rooms — **the highest dwell time of any venue category we sell**, and the catchment is regional, not local. |
| 2 | **Barnes Crossing District** | Super-regional mall + surrounding strip centers, 1.5M+ sq ft retail | **10–12** | Largest retail concentration in Northeast Mississippi. Strip-center independents are the target, not mall anchors. |
| 3 | **North Gloster Street** | Primary retail corridor | **8–10** | Steady daily traffic. Our own Tupelo marketing already names this corridor — the story is written, the screens just aren't there. |
| 4 | **Downtown Main Street / Fairpark District** | Civic + dining core | **6–8** | The walkable node. Oxford playbook again. |
| 5 | **Hwy 45 / West Main service corridors** | Auto, gyms, service businesses | **4–6** | Fill-in. |

**This track needs no permission from anyone.** No territory grant, no new rep, no new market
education — hosts already know the brand and the traction reports already exist. If DeSoto is the
land grab, Tupelo is the money sitting on the table.

## Competitive overlay — DeSoto Local

**Status: unknown, and it is the single biggest gap in this map.**

We know the operator (Desoto Local, Inc. / Brandy Faulkner, an N-Compass–NTV360 dealer) and their
described footprint ("restaurants to gyms, doctors' offices to auto repair" across DeSoto County).
We do **not** know which specific venues, how many licenses, or when contracts expire — and it is
not publicly discoverable. Their site 403s automated fetches and no aggregator publishes N-Compass
venue lists.

**Working assumption until the NTV360 export lands:** a small operator building DeSoto County starts
in **Southaven** (largest city, densest retail) and works outward along Goodman Road. That assumption
is *why* the phasing above puts Olive Branch and Hernando first — they are both the strongest
markets and the likeliest to be uncontested.

**Fill this section in the moment Don sends the license export.** Every node above gets a
contested/clear flag, and the phasing gets re-cut against reality.

## Open questions this map can't answer yet

1. **Venue-level competitive overlay** — needs the NTV360 license export from Don.
2. **Host economics in DeSoto** — our model is $0-cost hosting, not revenue share. Does that hold in
   a Memphis-DMA suburb where venues may have been offered rev-share by the incumbent?
3. **Rep coverage** — 65–80 DeSoto screens is not a remote-managed market. Who lives there?
4. **Hardware and install cost per screen** — the real price of "free" territory. Needed before
   agreeing to any build-out clause.
