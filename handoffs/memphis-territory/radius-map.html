Short answer: one real market, and it isn't in Mississippi. Tuscaloosa is 53 miles from Columbus — 116,477 people plus roughly 34,000 University of Alabama students. That is the only genuine second Oxford inside the radius, and Oxford is the build we have already proven at 75 screens. Everything else in range is either a small satellite of a market we already serve, or shrinking.

But the best move inside 60 miles isn't a new market at all. Columbus has 2 screens and West Point has 1 — while Lowndes County absorbs a $2.5 billion Steel Dynamics investment creating 1,000 jobs at an average wage of $93,000, plus a $90M Kloeckner aluminum plant and a $200M Aluminum Dynamics expansion. We are three screens deep in a county having the biggest industrial decade in its history.

Recommendation

1. Finish the Golden Triangle before opening anything new. Columbus and West Point are 3 screens between them against a $2.5B industrial build-out. No territory negotiation, no new rep, existing brand. Same argument as Tupelo's under-density, with a bigger tailwind.

2. Scout Tuscaloosa, don't commit to it. It is the biggest prize in range and the closest thing to a second Oxford we will find — but Impulse Digital Media is already running our exact model there, it crosses a state line, and it needs a rep who lives in it. Worth a trip and a competitive read, not a build decision yet.

3. Fold the satellites in opportunistically. Pontotoc, Fulton, New Albany, Amory, Aberdeen and Water Valley are all inside 25 miles of a market we already run.
Caution on Columbus itself. The city is shrinking — 22,507 people, down 6.2% since 2020, median household income $42,235 with a 21.3% poverty rate. The money is arriving in Lowndes County at $93,000 a job, not in the city's existing household base. Put the screens where those wages get spent, which may mean Starkville and the Highway 45 corridor more than downtown Columbus. That is the same lesson as Byhalia, and worth checking on the ground before spending.
