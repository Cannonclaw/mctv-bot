**Subject:** Free public-service screen time for the City of Oxford — 75 screens

---

Mayor Tannehill,

Following up on my text. I'm Creed Cannon — my wife Mary Michael and I own MCTV Elite
Advertising here in Oxford. We operate 75 indoor digital screens around town: restaurants
and bars on and around the Square, gyms, salons and barbershops, medical and dental waiting
rooms, retail counters.

We would like to give the City a permanent spot on all 75 of them, free.

What that means:

- One spot in the rotation on every Oxford screen, running four times an hour, every hour
  we are on. The City decides what runs in it.
- Priority for urgent messages — boil water notices, severe weather, road and lane
  closures, missing persons.
- We build the artwork at no charge, and we send a monthly report showing plays delivered
  and estimated impressions, so you can see what the space is actually doing.

There is no cost, no contract, and nothing for the Board to approve. It is the same
75-screen package we sell to advertisers for $1,300 a month.

The reason I think it is worth fifteen minutes: these screens reach people sitting in a
restaurant or a waiting room who are not looking at their phone and cannot scroll past.
That is a room the City's website and Facebook page do not reach — and it is exactly where
you want a boil water notice to land.

Could I get fifteen minutes with you, or with whoever handles communications for the City?
I know you are in the middle of budget season, so I am glad to wait until after that if
that is easier.

One-page summary attached.

Thank you,
