# City of Oxford — Partnership Outreach Brief

**Prepared for:** T. Creed Cannon
**Target:** Mayor Robyn Tannehill, City of Oxford (note the spelling — Robyn, with a Y)
**Status:** Opening text sent. Awaiting reply.
**Date:** 2026-07-31

---

## 1. The play

Lead by giving. Offer the City a permanent, free public-service slot on all 75 Oxford
screens, plus priority handling for urgent messages. Ask for nothing in the first meeting.

The offer is deliberately structured so that accepting it is easy: no cost means no budget
line, no purchase order, no procurement question, and nothing for the Board of Aldermen to
approve. The mayor's office can say yes on its own.

**What we are actually buying with it:** standing as a civic institution rather than a
vendor. That is worth more than the $15,600/year of donated inventory, because it de-risks
every host-venue and advertiser conversation we have in Oxford for the next three years.

---

## 2. Why now

At 20 screens we would be a small business asking a mayor for a favor. At **75 screens
across Oxford** — the Square, University Ave, Highway 6 — we are a distribution channel
the City does not currently own. That is a category change, and it is what earns a real
meeting instead of a polite one.

The specific gap we fill: the City's existing channels (website, Facebook, the paper) reach
people who are already looking. Our screens reach people who are **sitting still, not on
their phone, and unable to scroll past** — in restaurants, gyms, salons, and waiting rooms.

---

## 3. What the City gets, ranked by how much they will care

1. **Emergency and public-safety messaging.** Boil water notices, severe weather, road
   closures, missing persons. When Ward 3 loses water at 4pm, the people who need to know
   are sitting in a restaurant on Jackson Ave. This is the argument that makes a mayor lean
   forward. Lead with it.
2. **Event and tourism promotion.** Double Decker, the Christmas parade, Oxford Film
   Festival, Park Commission programming. Visit Oxford has a real mandate to fill rooms.
3. **Gameday and parking logistics.** Traffic, shuttles, garage capacity, move-in week.
   A perennial complaint that is perennially hard to communicate.
4. **Civic operations.** Election dates and polling places, public hearings, budget open
   houses, waste and recycling changes, board and commission recruitment.
5. **City hiring.** OPD, OFD, Public Works, Utilities. Screens in barbershops and gyms
   reach working-age Oxford residents better than a legal ad.

## 5. Guardrails

- **Nothing political, ever.** Free space to the City as an institution is clean. Anything
  touching a campaign — hers or anyone's — is a bright line. Say this out loud in the
  meeting; volunteering it builds trust instead of raising the question later.
- **Keep the donation separate from any future paid work.** A donation of ad space is not a
  procurement. If the City ever pays us for anything, that goes through purchasing rules on
  its own track. Do not let the two blur.
- **Assume the text is a public record.** Communications with a public official about public
  business can fall under the Mississippi Public Records Act. Not a reason to avoid texting —
  a reason to write everything as if it may be read aloud.
- **Do not over-promise the alert turnaround.** See below.

## 6. Open item — verify before the meeting

> **How fast can we actually get a new spot whitelisted onto all 75 Oxford licenses?**

This is the strongest single number we will say in that room, and the one we cannot wing.
Confirm the real turnaround through n-compass, then quote it — and quote it conservatively.
The one-pager currently prints **"same business day"** as a placeholder; change it with
`--alert-turnaround` once the true figure is known.

Under-promise here. A city that has been told "within the hour" and gets four hours during
an actual weather event will never trust the channel again.

---

## 7. How the meeting should go

1. **Talk for five minutes. Listen for fifteen.** The most valuable question in the room:
   *"What do you have the hardest time getting the word out about?"* Her answer is the pilot.
2. **Expect a hand-off to staff.** The mayor is the yes; the person who will actually use
   this daily is the City's communications/PIO staff. Being routed there is success, not a
   brush-off. Get the name and the direct line before leaving.
3. **Pilot one thing.** One message, one month, one measurable outcome.
4. **Report back with real numbers.** Plays, screens, estimated impressions — the same
   traction report our advertisers get. This is the genuine differentiator: nobody else
   donating space in this town can hand the City a data report on what it received. That
   report is what converts a favor into a standing partnership.
5. **Then, and only then, expand** — city buildings as host venues, Visit Oxford as a paid
   client, and a quote we can use.

## 9. What to carry into the room

- **The one-pager** (`build_onepager.py`) — one page, printed. Resist bringing the deck.
- **The sample City messages** (`build_psa_frames.py`) — four real frames at the network's
  actual 1920×1080, printed as a second page. Concrete beats abstract by a wide margin in
  fifteen minutes. **Print the labeled set** (the one with the SAMPLE MOCKUP bar): the
  clean frames are realistic enough that a photograph of one circulating outside the
  meeting could pass for a live alert, and a fake NWS warning in the wild is a legal
  problem, not just an embarrassment. The bar also does sales work — it says we treat
  alert integrity seriously before anyone asks.
- **The verified alert turnaround** from section 6.

Lead the second page with the boil water frame, not the tornado. The tornado is the
dramatic one, but the boil water notice is the one she has actually had to get out, more
than once, and it is where she will feel the gap in her current channels. Let her get to
the tornado frame herself.

Say out loud that no MCTV logo appears on any of them. It is a thirty-second point that
does a lot of work: it says the City's message is the City's message, that we know the
difference between a public-service frame and an advertisement, and that we are not
looking to get credit on screen. That is the whole partnership in one sentence.
